Research & Proposed Thoughts: 
The first and most critical improvement is the systematic exploitation of invertible rules. A logical rule is said to be invertible if the premises are provable if and only if the conclusion is provable. In the context of automated deduction, applying an invertible rule is always "safe" because it does not require backtracking. If the goal is provable, it remains provable after the application of an invertible rule. The inversion strategy replaces "don't-know non-determinism" (where the prover must guess which rule to apply) with "don't-care non-determinism" (where any invertible rule can be applied in any order). This significantly reduces the branching factor of the search.


Beyond simple inversion, the algorithm should implement Focusing. Developed by Andreoli, Focusing forces the prover to complete a chain of related inferences before moving to a new part of the sequent, which eliminates many redundant permutations of the same proof tree. This "backchaining" approach is particularly effective in logics with large numbers of hypotheses, such as those found in software verification.

The algorithm either reuses existing terms or creates a fresh one, which is essentially a guess. This is highly inefficient because most terms in the Herbrand universe are irrelevant to the proof.

The academic solution is the transition to a Free-Variable Sequent Calculus.
This symbolic approach transforms a "guessing" problem into a constraint satisfaction problem, which can be solved efficiently using standard unification algorithms like the Martelli-Montanari algorithm.

## 3. Key Theoretical Concepts

### 3.1 Sequent Calculus (LK')
The sequent calculus LK' is a formal proof system where:
- **Sequents** have the form Γ ⊢ Δ (antecedent ⊢ succedent)
- **Rules** are applied backwards from conclusion to premises
- The system is **sound** (if we can derive Γ ⊢ Δ, then Γ logically implies Δ) and **complete** (if Γ logically implies Δ, there exists a derivation)

### 3.2 Invertibility
A rule is **invertible** if the conclusion is provable if and only if the premises are provable. Invertible rules can be applied greedily without losing completeness.

### 3.3 Eigenvariable Condition
When applying ∀R or ∃L backwards, the introduced variable must be **fresh** — it must not appear free in any formula in the sequent. This prevents unsound proofs.

### 3.4 Herbrand's Theorem
A quantifier-free formula is satisfiable iff it is satisfiable in a Herbrand interpretation. This justifies enumerating ground terms when instantiating quantifiers.

### 3.5 Unification
First-order unification finds a substitution σ such that σ(s) = σ(t) for terms s and t. Key properties:
- **Most general unifier (MGU)** exists and is unique (up to renaming)
- **Occurs check** prevents infinite terms
- Robinson's algorithm runs in exponential time worst-case, but practical implementations use near-linear algorithms
